Context for the 2024 Study

2021 Research: The Study That Shaped This Work

Before EYLDI studied what effective leadership development looks like, it asked a simpler question: do ECE leaders have access to it at all? This 2021 study — EYLDI's founding research — found that they largely didn't, especially leaders from marginalized backgrounds. That gap is what the 2024 research set out to understand and address.

~500 ECE professionals surveyed 457 responses analyzed 27 companion interviews 2019–2021

What the 2021 Study Found

Leadership quality shapes everything else in this sector — but who leads doesn't match who's served

Who leads doesn't match who's served

Nationally, about 50% of children under age 5 in the U.S. are children of color — yet roughly 79% of U.S. early childhood center directors are white, and only 21% are Black, Indigenous, or people of color.

Sources: 2016 Early Childhood Workforce Index; CSCCE, UC Berkeley; Annie E. Casey Foundation Kids Count; McCormick Center, 2018 — as cited in EYLDI's 2021 report.

The sector already agreed this mattered

In EYLDI's 2021 survey of ECE professionals, 82% endorsed the importance of advancing leaders from marginalized backgrounds, and 71% said the sector currently has too few leaders with diverse identities and backgrounds.

Source: EYLDI, Diversify Early Childhood Leadership (2021).

2021 · Diversify Early Childhood Leadership

EYLDI's founding study — a national survey asking what's holding ECE leaders back.

  • ~500 ECE professionals surveyed (457 analyzed)
  • 27 companion interviews
  • 82% endorsed advancing leaders from marginalized backgrounds
  • 71% said the sector has too few leaders with diverse backgrounds
  • Key finding: leaders — especially from marginalized backgrounds — lacked access to developmental relationships with more experienced leaders
