EYLDI interviewed 17 Leader-Developers across 7 U.S. states to find out what effective developmental relationships look like in practice. This is that research, organized so you can use it in your own work.

Why This Matters

Great leadership doesn't just happen. Someone develops it.

EYLDI's first study, in 2021, found that ECE leaders — especially those from marginalized backgrounds — often lacked access to developmental relationships with more experienced leaders. The 2024 research picks up from there, asking what those relationships actually look like when they work. Read about the 2021 study →

🌱

A Leader-Developer invests

An experienced ECE leader builds a real developmental relationship with a rising leader — guiding, understanding, and enabling their growth.

→
🧭

A new leader grows

That rising leader becomes more confident and effective — and more representative of the communities ECE serves.

Children benefit

Because leadership quality is linked to program quality, children and families ultimately feel the difference.

About This Research

Who did this, and how

The Early Years Leadership Diversity Initiative (EYLDI) conducted 17 in-depth, Behavioral Event Interviews with Leader-Developers across 7 U.S. states between March and July 2024, to understand what effective developmental relationships actually look like in practice. This study builds directly on EYLDI's 2021 founding research.

17 Leader-Developer interviews Behavioral Event Interview protocol March–July 2024 7 U.S. states

Research led by Meredith Callanan, Candice McKinnon, PhD, and Dina Kapengut, PhD. Meet the full team on the About EYLDI page →
