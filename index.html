The Problem

Leadership quality shapes everything else in this sector — but the sector doesn't yet invest in it that way.

Early care and education quality is linked to leadership quality. Yet ECE lacks intentional, sustained investment in developing its leaders — and the leaders who do rise often don't reflect the communities, families, and children they serve.

Who leads doesn't match who's served

Nationally, about 50% of children under age 5 in the U.S. are children of color — yet roughly 79% of U.S. early childhood center directors are white, and only 21% are Black, Indigenous, or people of color.

Sources: 2016 Early Childhood Workforce Index; CSCCE, UC Berkeley; Annie E. Casey Foundation Kids Count; McCormick Center, 2018 — as cited in EYLDI's 2021 report.

The sector already agrees this matters

In EYLDI's original 2021 survey of ECE professionals, 82% endorsed the importance of advancing leaders from marginalized backgrounds, and 71% said the sector currently has too few leaders with diverse identities and backgrounds.

Source: EYLDI, Diversify Early Childhood Leadership (2021).

Why This Work Matters

Investing in one leader ripples across the whole sector

EYLDI's research treats leadership development as a chain, not a single event. Strengthen one link, and the effect carries all the way down to children and families.

🌱

A Leader-Developer invests

An experienced ECE leader builds a real developmental relationship with a rising leader — guiding, understanding, and enabling their growth.

→
🧭

A new leader grows

That rising leader becomes more confident and effective — and more representative of the communities ECE serves.

→
🏫

Programs strengthen

Better-led programs mean stronger teams, higher educator retention, and more consistent, higher-quality care across the sector.

→
👶

Children benefit

Because leadership quality is linked to program quality, the children and families in those classrooms ultimately experience the difference.

Who We Are

A research and advocacy initiative for ECE leadership

The Early Years Leadership Diversity Initiative (EYLDI) engages and strengthens Early Care & Education leaders as they work to advance rising leaders — with the goal of improving equitable access to quality early learning across the sector.

Skilled Leaders Unlock Human Potential

We invest in developing leaders who coach, mentor, and support others, expanding what is possible for individuals, teams, and communities through relational, skillful practice.

Representative Leadership Matters

We create pathways that elevate underrepresented leaders and ensure leadership reflects the communities served, strengthening belonging, decision-making, and collective impact.

🌱

Humble Inquiry

We listen deeply, question assumptions, remain open to new ideas from unexpected places, and are awed by what we don't know.

🤝

Strengths-Based Equity

We strive to recognize and nourish the personal strengths of future leaders through greater access to resources and opportunities and the elimination of barriers.

🌍

Inclusive Community

We affirm all identities and differences in our collective communities and workforce, and embrace cultures of belonging and collaboration that support the agency of others.

🧭

Responsibility

We hold ourselves responsible for weaving our values into all parts of our work — and seek to inspire leaders to remove barriers and champion those who influence change.

The Research

Two studies, one throughline

In 2021, EYLDI asked what leaders in ECE need to grow. In 2025/26, EYLDI studied what actually works.

2019–2021

Harvard Advanced Leadership Initiative fellowship

EYLDI's original research is conceived as part of Meredith Callanan's Senior Fellowship at Harvard's Advanced Leadership Initiative.

2020

The work begins

EYLDI launches a national research study to capture beliefs and barriers around leadership development and leadership diversity in ECE.

2021

Diversify Early Childhood Leadership

~500 ECE professionals surveyed, plus 27 interviews. Key insight: leaders — especially from marginalized backgrounds — lacked access to developmental relationships with more experienced leaders. Read the 2021 study →

2024

17 Behavioral Event Interviews

EYLDI interviews Leader-Developers across 7 U.S. states, March–July, to understand what effective developmental relationships actually look like in practice.

2025/26

Leaders Developing Leaders

EYLDI publishes its five-competency framework — the current, most complete chapter of this research. Read the 2025/26 study →
